Less hunting. More watching.

Three simple steps.

  1. 01

    Record your game.

    Turn recording on and choose whole games, clips only, or a key to start when you want. Picture and sound settings stay in your hands.

  2. 02

    Watch it back.

    Open a game, play its recording and jump to an event when the video is lined up with the game clock. Slow down or step through a frame.

  3. 03

    Keep the good bit.

    Choose the start and end, preview it, then save a clip. Or revisit an automatic clip around a recorded fight.

The useful details

Before you ask.

How does a recording find its game?

Rift's own recordings can store the game ID and the game clock at recording start. When that information is available, Rift uses it to pair the recording and line up events.

The app can also look for compatible MP4s in the local Rift and Ascent recording folders. Other recordings may be paired by their time and length; that is an estimate, not recognition of the footage. You can choose a different file yourself. Your explicit choice takes priority.

For a compatible local MP4 or AVI, ordinary playback works without recorded events. Manual frame alignment is available with a supported event. Cuts and pauses can make an estimated offset wrong; no pixels are analysed. Riot .rofl files are not videos this player can open.

Windows media can occasionally stall when opening or playing a file. The app has bounded recovery, but the cause and exact file-release timing remain unresolved. A successful demonstration isn't a guarantee for every machine or recording.

What gets recorded?

Recording is opt-in. Choose whole games, clips only, always-ready clips between games, or recording started by a key. Past games don't gain a video retroactively.

The current default captures the whole screen League is on, which can include other apps, notifications and pop-ups. Between-games clips also capture the screen. Review the capture and sound choices before turning it on. Audio capture and playback sound are separate settings.

Recordings and saved clips live on your PC. Storage limits can remove older eligible recordings; favourites are protected by the current retention setting. Choose your folder and limits in Settings.

Does the score tell me what I did wrong?

No. The score combines recorded match statistics using role-aware weights, and the app shows its reasons and the role used. It is not an expert diagnosis, rank prediction or proof of a good decision.

Events such as kills, deaths and assists help you find a place to watch. The video is where you can examine the play. Nothing asks you to compare two arbitrary deaths or finish a reflection before watching a game.

What about runes, builds and the live page?

The private app can import League-client rune and summoner-spell recommendations in supported champion select. Those are client recommendations, not Rift-owned meta analysis. Third-party item imports remain paused.

A live teams page and experimental overlay exist. Enemy-marking hotkeys failed the last live check and are labelled unreliable in the app. There are no enemy ultimate timers, automatic gameplay or promised live tactical coaching. A proposed PowerSpike connection is not an available feature.

Where does my data go?

This website candidate has no forms, accounts, analytics, cookies or browser storage. It loads only its local styles, logo and bundled fonts. It does not contact Riot or scan your computer.

The desktop app stores match records, review choices, recordings and clips locally. The recordings feature creates media files; watching an existing recording does not upload or modify it. Match files and exported backups are not anonymous or encrypted. A cloud-synced folder can copy them off your PC.

The desktop app contacts Riot services for supported client/API features and public artwork. An internet request exposes ordinary connection information such as your IP address. The privacy notice has the details; a verified contact address will be added there before anything is offered.

Can I download it or sign up?

Not yet. The private developer build is not a customer-distribution system. It uses a developer-entered API key for testing; a customer release must keep shared production keys out of the desktop binary and needs the appropriate Riot approval.

No production API key approval or public release is claimed. Terms, Privacy Policy, verified contact, host and reviewer access remain unfinished. Do not send a Riot password or API key through this page.
